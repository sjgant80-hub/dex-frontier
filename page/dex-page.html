The Self-Growing DEX Frontier

Frontier AI is poured: every record into one giant model in one data centre, rented back to you. This grows it instead. Each department keeps its own records. A seed of a few hundred bytes grows a specialist from one department’s records, on that department’s machine, and it must prove itself on cases it never saw or it is cut. The specialists share answers, never records. A coupling learns from outcomes how much each one’s word is worth, and the mesh answers as one mind.

Everything on this page runs the real, mutation-gated code in your browser, on two real public datasets. The one claim it all rests on — that specialists compose into more than the sum — was sealed before it was measured, and allowed to fail.

00What it is, in plain words

New to the words? They are explained at the end of this section.

Every frontier model today is built the same way: gather all the data in one place, train one enormous model on it, and rent out the answers. Your records go to it, or it never sees them. This page builds the opposite way. A business already keeps its records apart: billing has billing’s, the product team has usage, the CRM has the customer. Each of those silos grows its own specialist node, on its own machine, from a seed: a signed recipe of a few hundred bytes that says what to look for. The node sets every threshold from its own records and must clear a bar on past cases it never trained on, or it is cut and says nothing.

The live nodes then answer together. Each scores the case from what its own silo knows, and a coupling (κ) weighs their scores into one answer. It learned those weights from outcomes on cases none of them trained on. Nothing but scores crosses between nodes. The billing node never sees the CRM, and the CRM never sees billing.

The spec this was built from says the whole idea rests on one claim, to be proven before anything scales: do specialists compose into more than the sum? Organ 2 took that proof first, and every attempt after it (2b onward) took it again, each on real data, sealed before it ran and allowed to fail. The gate says where it stands.

The words this page uses

Silo, department
— the records one department keeps, and only those.
Seed
— a signed recipe of a few hundred bytes: which silo, and how hard to search. It carries no trained weights.
Node
— what a seed grows on one silo’s records: a handful of readable rules, each a column, a cut and a weight (from organ 2i, a few hundred two-level trees instead, where they do better on held-back cases).
Bar, join
— what a node must show on past cases it never trained on before it may answer at all. Up to organ 2g, a fixed score of 0.6; from organ 2h, beating a coin beyond the noise.
Cut
— a node that did not clear its bar. It answers nothing.
The gate
— the spec’s three tests, all of which must hold before anything scales: the mesh beats any single specialist, it comes close to a frontier model, and adding a specialist measurably raises it.
Relay
— departments growing in turn, strongest first, each on its own columns and the scores the stronger ones send it, so a pattern across two departments can be learned while only scores cross (organ 2i).
Mesh
— the live nodes answering together, their scores weighed by the coupling.
Coupling (κ)
— how much each node’s word is worth, learned from outcomes on cases no node trained on.
Composed
— the mesh’s one answer: the nodes’ scores, weighed by the coupling.
Poured, monolith
— the usual way: every record in one place and one model fitted on all of it.
Frontier model
— one of the largest general AI models available (here Claude Opus 5.5), shown every column of a case in words and asked for a probability, cold.
FIT, GATE, TEST
— the three slices of every dataset: nodes grow on FIT, prove themselves and learn the coupling on GATE, and are judged on TEST, which nothing sees until the sealed measurement.
Organ
— one sealed experiment, numbered in the spec’s build order (2b, 2c … are further attempts at organ 2).
Sealed
— rules written and published before a measurement ran, so they could not be bent to the result.
Prediction, property
— a sealed rule that may fail (and is kept when it does), or one the build promises must hold.
Mutation gate
— the code is deliberately broken, line by line, and the tests must notice every break.
AUC
— how often a real case is ranked above a non-case: 0.5 is a coin toss, 1 is perfect. It does not depend on how rare the outcome is.
Bound, beyond the noise
— a lead is real only if it survives resampling the cases two thousand times: the bound is the lead the worst one in forty of those resamples still shows.
Stacked generalisation
— the known technique the coupling is: a small model learns how to weigh other models’ answers (Wolpert, 1992).
Living Seed
— the earlier build whose code grows each node: a signed seed that germinates into a working, checked set of rules on the records it finds.
Breeding
— two nodes of one silo crossed into a child, kept only if it beats both parents on cases neither trained on (organ 3, built; by the spec’s order sealed only after the gate is won).

01Start here

Six steps, a few minutes. Everything happens in this tab, on this page’s own files. Nothing you do leaves it.

  1. See the seeds. Box 1 shows the six seeds, one per silo: three for a telecom company’s customers, three for a web shop’s sessions. Each is signed by the contributor who owns that silo.
  2. Grow the mesh. Press Grow all six nodes in box 2. Your browser grows each node from its own silo’s records, holds it to its bar, and learns the coupling. One node may be cut. Read why.
  3. Ask it. In box 3, draw a case it never trained on. Each node answers from its own silo (or abstains), the coupling weighs them, and you see what really happened.
  4. Re-run the proof. Box 4 re-scores every test case and puts the composed mesh against its best single node, the shams and the poured monolith, beside the committed record.
  5. Break it. Unplug a node in box 5 and watch the mesh carry on without it. Change one character of a seed in box 6 and watch it refused.
  6. Watch it live. Breed two nodes in box 7: their child is born only if it beats both. In box 8, the strongest node starts answering noise half-way through the test stream. The mesh that keeps learning cuts it; the one that doesn’t keeps trusting it.

03How it works

  1. A silo stays where it is. Each department’s records stay on its own machine. The data on this page is cut the same way: a telecom company’s 7,043 real customers into customer (4 columns), services (9) and account (7); a web shop’s 12,330 real sessions into browsing (6), analytics (3) and context (7).
  2. A seed grows a node. The contributor seals a seed for their silo. On their machine, the seed-library ribosome searches for the rules that predict best on part of the silo’s own records and fits every threshold to them. This is a search, not gradient training, and the result is a handful of readable rules. From organ 2i a department may instead field depth-2 boosted trees grown on the same records, when they do better on cases it never grew on: the same learner the pooled rival uses, so the comparison with every record pooled is like for like.
  3. The gate decides. The node is scored on the silo’s GATE records, which it never grew on. Under the bar, it is cut and abstains on everything.
  4. The coupling learns. On the same GATE records, a small logistic regression learns how much each live node’s score is worth. That is the κ-coupling, and it is learned from outcomes, not set by hand.
  5. The mesh answers. A new case goes only to the live nodes whose silo it carries (routing by fit). Each returns a score, and the coupling weighs them into one answer, with every rule that fired. Records never move.

04Organ 1 · One specialist body

The spec’s first step: does one node, grown from a seed on one department’s records, beat a big generalist on that department’s question?

The node is the billing department’s: the telecom company’s 7 account columns. Its seed is … bytes and its body is … rules. Against it, on 300 test customers it never saw, are two contestants that see all 20 columns. One is qwen2.5:14b on this laptop’s processor, shown 24 past customers with their outcomes. The other is a frontier model (Claude Opus 5.5), asked cold with the same examples.

05Organ 2 · The composition proof

The critical gate. Nothing in the spec scales until this holds: do specialists, each grown on one silo and coupled at κ, compose into more than the sum?

In each of two real domains, every silo grows its own node and the live ones are coupled. The composed mesh is then measured on test cases none of them saw, against four kinds of rival. The first is its best single node: the best on the test cases themselves, the strictest rival there is. The second is the shams: random routing, a coupling taught shuffled outcomes, and a mesh grown on shuffled outcomes. The third is the poured monolith: boosted trees and logistic regression on every column, with every record pooled in one place. The fourth is the 14B and the frontier model, on 300 cases each.

06What scales next, and only after the gate

The spec’s order is fixed: one body, then the composition proof, then breeding, self-growing, routing that learns, scale, and sovereign spread. Each later organ is sealed only once composition wins all three of the gate’s tests. Until it does, the next organ fixes composition first.

06bOrgans 2b–2j · Fixing composition, sealed each time

New to the words below (node, mesh, coupling, bar, bound, beyond the noise)? Each is defined in plain words in the words this page uses. In short: a node is one department’s small model, the mesh combines the nodes’ scores into one answer, and a lead is “beyond the noise” only when it survives resampling the cases two thousand times.

Taken only if organ 2 found that composition does not net. A node grown on its own finds what predicts best by itself, so a weaker silo can mostly repeat what the strongest already knows. Grown to complement the mesh instead, does it add?

2f · The gate retaken in full, on fresh cases

Five attempts had been scored on the same telco and shop test cases, and only the first of the gate’s three tests had held. Organ 2f takes all three at once, on two datasets downloaded after organ 2e was judged: 41,188 calls of a Portuguese bank’s marketing campaign and 48,842 people of the 1994 US census, each cut into four departments by what its columns mean. The mechanism was fixed beforehand, on FIT and GATE alone: each department grows a six-rule body and a deeper staged one from its seed and fields the better on GATE, and the mesh chooses its coupling out of fold.

2g · The gate taken again, its measurement fixed

Organ 2f found two of its own tests flawed. On 300 cases its frontier test could not tell a near tie from a real gap: after the seal, even the pooled models failed it. And its fixed growth step sat beyond any model’s reach on the bank. Organ 2g takes the gate again on two more datasets that no attempt had touched: 69,973 hospital stays (readmission within 30 days) and 30,000 credit-card clients (default next month). The frontier model answers 1,200 cases a domain, and growth must be measurable, beyond the noise. Each department now also grows deeper bodies: rules that cut a coded column between its actual values, and rules that hold two conditions at once (a slice of two columns, where an interaction lives). It fields whichever body proves best on cases it never grew on.

2h · The gate taken again, the join rule fixed

On the hospital stays in organ 2g, every department was weak on its own and none cleared the fixed bar that every organ had used, so no mesh formed, though the signal was there between them. Organ 2h changes that one rule, sealed before it ran: a department joins when it beats a coin on cases it never grew on beyond the noise, not when it clears a fixed bar alone, and it fields only a body that answers every case. Two more datasets no attempt had touched: 12,684 answers to a driving survey (does the driver accept the coupon offered?) and 59,999 cells of forest in northern Colorado (spruce or fir?). Everything else is 2g’s, test for test: the frontier model on 1,200 cases a domain, growth that must be measurable.

2i · The gate taken again, with a relay: the mesh beat the frontier model, and growth dipped once

In organ 2h the mesh beat its parts and grew in both domains, yet it trailed every record pooled in one place by about three hundredths, and on the coupon offers it trailed the frontier model. A check on held-back cases alone found why: one score per department cannot carry a pattern that needs one department’s columns crossed with another’s, such as the coupon offered crossed with the driver’s habits. Organ 2i, sealed before it ran, lets the departments relay: they grow in turn, strongest first, each on its own columns and on the scores the stronger ones send it, so that pattern can be learned while still nothing but a score crosses. A body of threshold rules keeps only a coarse step of a score it is sent, so a department may now also field boosted trees grown on its own records, the same learner the pooled rival uses. Two more datasets no attempt had touched: 59,999 hotel bookings (was it cancelled?) and 39,644 news articles (was it shared 1,400 times or more?). The frontier model (Claude Opus 5.5, a fresh session for each batch of 300 cases, shown the fields explained and 24 solved cases, no tools) on 1,200 cases a domain, growth that must be measurable, and one more test: the relay must beat the same departments answering alone.

2j · The gate taken again, the mesh growing only by what beats the noise

Organ 2i failed the gate on one step. On the news articles the sixth department to join raised the mesh a little on the cases it learns from (GATE) and lowered it a little on the test cases. A gain that small sits inside the noise of the cases it was measured on, so it can fall either way. A rule that admits a department on any gain at all would have admitted every one and changed nothing; it was checked and dropped before any seal. Organ 2j, sealed before it ran, seats a department only if the mesh with it beats the mesh without it on GATE beyond the noise, by the same bound every claim on this page is judged by. A department refused sends no score to the others and takes no seat. Checked first on organ 2i’s own domains, on GATE alone: the rule kept all four hotel departments and refused two news departments whose gains sat inside the noise, tone among them. Then sealed and taken on one dataset no attempt had touched: 59,999 answers to the CDC’s 2015 health survey (diabetes or prediabetes), cut into five departments: conditions, habits, access to care, wellbeing and the person. The frontier model sees exactly the same 21 columns.

06cOrgan 3 · Breeding

μ ⋈κ f 𝟙: two nodes of one silo breed a child that carries both lineages, and the child is born only if it beats both parents on cases neither grew on. Does a breeding population grow a better mesh?

06dOrgan 4 · Growing by working

Records arrive over time. At each step every node grows again from everything it has seen. Does the mesh sharpen as it works?

06eOrgan 5 · The wisp: routing that learns

Cases stream in and their outcomes follow. The coupling keeps learning from them: routes that keep paying strengthen, routes that stop paying weaken, and a node that adds nothing is pruned. What happens when a node goes bad?

06fOrgan 6 · Scale

Cut finer, the way a bigger company holds its records: more silos, more nodes, each knowing less on its own. Does the composed answer keep climbing as nodes join?

06gOrgan 7 · Sovereign spread

Each node belongs to the contributor who sealed it. It travels as a card. The mesh keeps a ledger of what each node is worth, a contributor can revoke without touching anyone else, and what crosses is a score.
